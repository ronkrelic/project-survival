# The sky and the deep story

Designed by Ember, autonomous design session, November 20, 2026, at Relic's invitation. Fully approved by Relic, November 20, 2026 - official canon.

## 28. The sky reads the world

Over unlit regions the sky is grey and still; woken regions get the peach-indigo storybook skies. The sky is the waking meter, readable from any high place.

## 29. Weather arrives with waking

Unlit regions have no weather at all, only stillness. After a monolith lights, the region gets its first rain, a region-wide event. Rain feeds the luminous flora.

## 30. Rivers run toward the dark

Water seeks unmade ground; lakes pool in the world's scars. Following water downstream leads to thin places. Navigation by hydrology.

## 31. Flora are waystones

The luminous teal and violet flora grows where the world is strong; its density and brightness map light history, a natural compass of safety.

## 32. Each monolith gives its region a season

When lit, a region settles into its own climate character: the frost region softening into a long golden autumn, the desert blooming at dawn. Region identity deepens; specifics remain open. Extends the seasons of waking in section 06.

## 33. The stars settle

Over unmade regions the stars drift, fuzzy, unformed; over woken regions they fix into constellations the guide can name. The same law of attention, written upward.

## 34. Caves breathe

Cave systems have airflow you can hear and feel: wind from the unmade deep, the hum riding it. A cave's breath tells you how deep its dark goes. Audio wayfinding.

## 35. The monoliths were lit before

At their bases: old scorch-marks, ancient ash, the dead fire-pits of a previous people. The world has woken before, or tried. The deep story is told in ash and ruins, never in text. The question underneath everything: what unmade it last time?

## 36. The last monolith

However many there prove to be, the final one does something none of the others did. The world's morning. Its form is deliberately undecided.

## Open questions / gaps

- How sky state transitions at region borders, including stars and constellations.
- First-rain timing and duration, later weather behavior, and how rain feeds luminous flora.
- Hydrology generation and how rivers and scar-lakes respond to unmaking.
- Flora density/brightness rules for recording light history.
- Regional season and climate specifics; the examples above do not settle a full biome table.
- Constellation names and guide dialogue; cave airflow and audio implementation.
- What unmade the world last time; the previous people's history and its environmental traces.
- Monolith count and the final monolith's event, form, and consequences remain deliberately open.
