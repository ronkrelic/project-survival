# Cosmology and the waking world

All of the following direction was decided by Relic and Ember in conversation, November 20, 2026. Implementation details flagged below remain open.

## The world is newly waking

The world is newly made, newly waking. Embers are what it makes first: every player arrives carrying a spark of the world's own first fire, and that spark is a someone — the spirit guide. The guide is not assigned to the player; the guide is part of what the player is.

Renaming the guide at character creation is not naming a helper. It is naming the part of yourself that talks.

## Darkness unmakes

Deep dark — unlit night and deep caves — slowly dissolves blocks back into nothing. Light holds the world together. A campfire is a claim stake, not cozy set dressing. The player's carried ember is a ring of safety around them. Night is genuinely dangerous without any monsters.

## The guide remembers

The guide remembers the player's journey and references past events contextually: a near-death in a canyon might be mentioned weeks later when passing it again. This is a memory/flag system, not scripted lines.

## How the guide teaches

The guide never hands over a recipe list. He watches what the player carries and nudges discovery: “you've never tried those two together.” The recipe book fills through the player's own discovery. If stuck, the player can ask “what could I make with this?” and receive a hint, not an answer. The guide is a teacher who refuses to do your homework.

## The edges of the world are unfinished

Far from any light or attention, terrain is half-formed: hazy, with blocks fuzzy at the seams. The world is still being made there, and it is made by attention — lighting it and walking it settles it. Infinite procedural generation becomes myth: the procedural fade at distance is cosmology instead of a technical trick.

## Death relights you

The ember cannot be unmade. On death, the player wakes at the last fire they lit: campfire, hearth, or monolith. What they were carrying stays where they fell, in the dark, slowly unmaking until recovered. Death is not a punishment screen; it is a journey home in the dark.

## The world sounds thin

Near newly-made or nearly-unmade places there is a faint hum, barely there. The guide goes quiet near it, or says something quiet. Audio is cosmology; caves gain a dread that is not monsters.

## Monoliths and the seasons of waking

The leaning monoliths in the concept art are ancient and dark. Lighting them is the world's deep goal: each is a waystone and checkpoint, and each lit monolith changes its region. Weather softens, new flora blooms, and possibly new blocks become real that were not before.

The world's story is told in what lighting it does to the land, not in text dumps. In multiplayer this is a shared project no one is forced into: a valley of camps, and on the ridge, one monolith burning again.

## Threat philosophy

The primary threat is darkness and unmaking, not monsters. Combat and enemy design is deliberately deferred and may never be monster-based. Whether any combat exists at all remains open.

## Open questions / gaps

- Unmaking rate, light radii per source, which light sources count, and whether player builds can be affected.
- Guide trigger and memory architecture, and dialogue content.
- Recipe-teaching implementation details within the decided hint-led discovery approach.
- How distance and attention are measured, and terrain-settling mechanics.
- Dropped-item decay rates and whether anything else is lost on death.
- Audio design beyond the faint hum and the guide's quiet response.
- Number of monoliths, region-change specifics, and what new content unlocks; whether new blocks become real.
- Whether any combat exists at all; combat and enemy design remain deferred.
